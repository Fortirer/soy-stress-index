# US competitors and partners

Researched in October 2026 from public sources only. None of these companies publishes accuracy against county yields or a price list. Check the details again before you use them in a pitch, and ask directly in conversations.

## Summary

| Company | What it sells | To whom | What it means for Turgor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Agrograph** (Madison, WI) | Field-level yield estimates from satellite + machine learning; yield-volatility scores | Crop insurers, lenders, grain companies | Closest US match on lenders and insurers; field yield data from customers is their moat |
| **CeresAI** (ex-Ceres Imaging) | Field-level agronomic and risk insights from aerial and satellite imagery | Large farms, lenders, insurers | Well funded (Series D, 2024), broad crops; less focused on in-season weather stress |
| **EarthDaily** (EarthDaily Agro) | Productivity risk score, planting validation, crop ID, portfolio alerts, parametric insurance design | Lenders, insurers, ag co-ops | Owns a satellite constellation; strongest data platform. Possible partner: Turgor as a stress layer on their data |
| **Arbol** | Parametric weather risk and reinsurance | Insurers, corporates | Possible buyer of a soybean trigger (US and Brazil) |
| **Climate FieldView** (Bayer) and other farm platforms | Field data, imagery, agronomy for growers | Farmers, agronomists | Not direct competitors in lending and insurance; a possible data channel |
| **Gro Intelligence** | Crop and climate data platform | Traders, governments, food companies | **Closed in mid-2024** after raising $85 M (Series B 2021). A former employee cited a product–market mismatch. A warning for broad data platforms without a clear buyer |

## Agrograph

- Founded by Mutlu Ozdogan (CEO, a University of Wisconsin remote-sensing professor) and James O'Brien. A gener8tor accelerator graduate.
- Raised a $500 k seed round from the Idea Fund of La Crosse in 2018 ([AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/agrograph-raises-500k-in-seed-fundin-to-predict-crop-yields)). Later rounds were not checked.
- Estimates the yield of each field with machine learning on satellite imagery and field data, with a reported 5–15% error, back 10–15 years. Target customers are insurers (claims), lenders (yield volatility) and grain companies.
- **Where Turgor differs:** it is in-season and stage-based (it says *why* a field is losing yield: water deficit and heat at flowering and seed fill), it is validated in public against county yields with confidence intervals, and it runs in Brazil too.
- **Where Agrograph is ahead:** field-level yield data from customers, US relationships and a product already sold.

## CeresAI

- Rebranded from Ceres Imaging in September 2024 with a Series D led by Remus Capital. The amount was not disclosed ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/ceresai-series-d-funding-raised-to-help-insurers-reduce-farming-risks)).
- Sells field-level insights to underwrite policies and manage lending risk, across 40 crop types ([CeresAI risk solutions](https://ceres.ai/risk-solutions)).
- **For Turgor:** the company competes for the same insurer and lender budget, but with a broad, imagery-first product. Turgor's angle is narrower and deeper: soybean physiology, in-season timing and a published backtest.

## EarthDaily

- Its insurance and lending offer includes a productivity risk score, planting validation, crop identification, crop-health and drought monitoring, yield-based lending support, parametric insurance design and portfolio alerts ([EarthDaily](https://earthdaily.com/insurance-and-lending)). No customers are named on the page.
- **For Turgor:** this is the strongest platform competitor. It is also a natural partner, since Turgor's stress index could be one of the layers delivered on their imagery.

## Arbol

- See the Brazil battlecard (`docs/negocio/concorrentes_pt.md`): parametric risk, expanding in Latin America under Hector Ibarra.
- In the US, the pitch is a soybean trigger with lower basis risk than July–August rainfall. Make it only if the Illinois numbers support it (see `midwest/README.md`).

## What the US market tells us

1. **The US is crowded and well served by data.** Federal crop insurance covers 72% of Illinois soybean acres (RMA 2024). Yield-based policies (RP, YP, ECO, SCO) already pay on measured yield, so a weather trigger has less room than in Brazil.
2. **The buyer is narrower:** private supplemental products, reinsurers, lenders watching a loan book in season, and grain merchandisers forecasting supply.
3. **The best use of Illinois for Turgor is as evidence**, not as the first market: the method works on another continent, on public data, with honest limits. This helps sell in Brazil and to global buyers such as Arbol and the reinsurers.
