# Founding in the US: status, funding and support

*Not legal or immigration advice.* This page lists the questions to ask and the programs to check. Only your visa sponsor (UIUC International Student and Scholar Services) and an immigration attorney can tell you what you may do.

## 1. Your status comes first

You are a visiting PhD researcher at UIUC, still registered at USP. US visa rules for visiting students and scholars (usually J-1, sometimes F-1 or B-1) **limit work, including work for your own company**. University offices commonly distinguish between:

- **Passive ownership**: owning shares, often allowed.
- **Active work**: building the product, selling or managing the company, which usually needs work authorization.

They also point to specific routes: J-1 research scholars can work for a company only when it is the approved research site, and some universities run entrepreneur-in-residence programs ([WashU OISS](https://oiss.washu.edu/entrepreneurial-activities/); [IFP on the J-1 research scholar](https://ifp.org/j-1-research-scholar-visa/)).

**Questions for UIUC ISSS (send before any US company or customer work):**

1. With my current visa category and sponsor, may I own shares in a US or Brazilian startup?
2. May I talk to potential US customers, pitch at events or enter hackathons as the founder of a Brazilian company?
3. May I accept a prize or a grant (for example I-Corps) while on this visa?
4. If I wanted to work on a US company before my stay ends, what would be required (sponsor approval, a different category)?

**A safe default until you have answers:** do research, customer interviews for learning, hackathons and pitch events. Keep the company in Brazil (see issue #17), and do not sign US customer contracts or take US payment.

## 5. Recommended path

1. Email ISSS with the four questions above.
2. Apply to **I-Corps** if eligible: it pays for exactly the interviews you need, now in the US too.
3. Use the hackathon (13–15 Nov) and MaRS (17–18 Nov) to meet US data or business people who could be US cofounders.
4. Keep the operating company in Brazil (PIPE, issue #20). Treat the US as evidence and a later market.
