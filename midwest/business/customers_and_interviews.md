## Interview plan (10 problem interviews, then 5 demos)

Reuse the method in the private interview tracker (Portuguese). Run it in English for the US.

**Problem interview (30 min, no product):**

1. What is your role, and where does weather risk enter your decisions?
2. Tell me about 2012 (or 2023's June flash drought). What happened in your book?
3. When did you know there would be losses? How did you find out?
4. What do you use today: crop condition reports, NASS forecasts, Drought Monitor, satellite vendors, field visits?
5. What does that cost per season, in money and time?
6. What is the hardest part?
7. Who else should I talk to?

**Demo (30 min):** show the Illinois 2012 replay map and the validation table (`midwest/README.md`). Then ask:

- What would you have done differently on 1 August 2012?
- Who approves a data purchase, and when is the budget set?
- The four price questions (too cheap, cheap, expensive, too expensive), in US$ per acre per season, or per loan or policy if that is how they think.

**Rules:**

- Ask about the past, not what they would do.
- Listen 80% of the time.
- Ask for numbers.
- No pitching in problem interviews.

I study how drought and heat at flowering and seed fill drive soybean yield loss, and I've built a public stress index from satellite and weather data, validated on Brazilian and Illinois counties.
